-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. VALIDATION SCHEME                                                [D18]
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Stratified 5-fold cross-validation, shuffled, random_state = 42.
- The SAME folds are used for every model (ev.CV), so comparisons are fair.
- Preprocessing is inside each pipeline, so it is re-fitted on each training
  fold (no leakage between folds).
- Hyperparameter tuning (Step 5) also uses these folds, on training data only.
- The test set is used ONCE at the end (ev.evaluate_test).

  Fold check - stratification worked:
    fold   train rows   val rows   val yes   val yes-rate
    1-4      26,352       6,588       742       11.26%
    5        26,352       6,588       743       11.28%


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. METRICS                                                          [D18]
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  PRIMARY:    PR-AUC (average precision) - ranking quality focused on the
              rare "yes" class. Random guessing = yes-rate (~0.113).
  BUSINESS:   Top-20% capture - share of all subscribers found by calling
              only the 20% highest-scored clients. Random calling = 20%.
  SECONDARY:  ROC-AUC (0.5 = random; not affected by the yes-rate),
              recall, precision, F1 (for "yes"), balanced accuracy.
  NOT USED to choose models: accuracy (misleading - see below).
  Threshold-free metrics (PR-AUC, ROC-AUC, top-20%) are used to compare
  models; recall/precision/F1 use the default 0.5 threshold until the
  threshold is tuned in evaluation.

5. TIME-DRIFT CHECK - shuffled CV vs forward-in-time CV             [D12]
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Training rows by (reconstructed) year:
    2008: 22,116 rows, 4.92% yes | 2009: 9,159 rows, 19.19% yes |
    2010: 1,665 rows, 51.89% yes

  Forward-in-time CV (TimeSeriesSplit, 5 folds - train on earlier rows,
  validate on the next block):
    fold  train rows  val period  val yes%   ROC-AUC LR / RF   PR-AUC lift LR / RF
    1        5,490    2008          4.74      0.520 / 0.513     1.03 / 1.03
    2       10,980    2008          6.54      0.526 / 0.504     1.07 / 1.15
    3       16,470    2008          5.57      0.439 / 0.415     0.93 / 0.82
    4       21,960    2008-2009    12.68      0.667 / 0.623     1.61 / 1.52
    5       27,450    2009-2010    35.17      0.421 / 0.696     0.89 / 1.50

  Comparison:
                       ROC-AUC shuffled  ROC-AUC forward (mean / min)  PR-AUC lift shuffled / forward
    LogReg (untuned)        0.788            0.515 / 0.421                 3.88 / 1.11
    RandomForest (untuned)  0.796            0.550 / 0.415                 4.12 / 1.20

  FINDING: predicting a FUTURE period from the past is far harder than
  predicting clients from the same periods (near-random in most folds).

  WHY (checked in the data):
  1. The strongest client signal did not exist yet in 2008. It was the
     bank's first campaign period: only 2.7% of 2008 clients had been
     contacted before (32.1% in 2009, 59.0% in 2010); poutcome = success
     was 0.1% in 2008 vs 31.5% in 2010. Validation folds 1-2 contain NO
     previously contacted clients.
  2. Every validation block contains months the model never saw in training
     (e.g. fold 1 trains on May only, validates on Jun/Jul; fold 4 meets
     Mar/Apr/Dec for the first time). One-hot encoding ignores unseen months.
  3. Economic values move outside the training range (fold 4 validation
     euribor 1.27-4.02 vs training 4.02-5.04; fold 5 0.63-1.29). Logistic
     Regression extrapolates linearly and fails badly in fold 5 (ROC-AUC
     0.42); Random Forest is more robust there (0.70).
  NOTE: early folds are a harsh test - they train on only 1-4 months of data.

  CONCLUSION
  - Much of the shuffled-CV skill comes from period / economic conditions
    and prior-campaign history, not only from stable client characteristics.
  - The model can NOT be trusted under new economic conditions without
    retraining on recent data.

  DECISION (D12 closed)
  - Shuffled stratified CV remains the scheme for comparing models: all
    models face identical conditions, and it matches how the hold-out test
    set was drawn.
  - Time drift is reported as a KEY LIMITATION, with recommendations:
    retrain regularly on the most recent campaigns, monitor performance,
    and use the model for ranking within a campaign period.
  - Added to Step 6 (evaluation): check the final model's ranking quality
    separately within each year of the test set.
