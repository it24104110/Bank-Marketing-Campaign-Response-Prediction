    alone looks less important than the group really is.

3. DIRECTION OF EFFECTS (SHAP + LR odds ratios)          [fig18, fig20, fig21]
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Numeric (RF SHAP dependence):
  - euribor3m: below ~1.5 -> pushes towards YES (+0.05 to +0.15);
               4-5 -> pushes towards NO (-0.05 to -0.10).
  - nr.employed: below 5,100 -> YES (+0.10 to +0.19); 5,100+ -> NO.
  - age: U-SHAPE - under ~30 and over ~60 push towards yes; 35-55 towards
         no. (The model learned the non-linear pattern seen in the EDA.)
  - campaign: 1-3 calls neutral; 5+ calls clearly negative (fatigue).

6. RESPONSIBLE-AI CHECK
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(a) Group outcomes - RF at the Capacity-20% threshold (0.424)
    age     clients  actual yes%  selected%  recall%  precision%
    17-25      328      18.9        46.0       90.3     37.1
    26-30    1,106      13.8        28.0       70.6     34.8
    31-40    3,312      10.9        16.7       61.7     40.1
    41-50    2,021       7.5        12.5       54.3     32.5
    51-60    1,302       9.4        17.1       61.8     34.2
    61-70       92      41.3        98.9      100.0     41.8
    71+         75      54.7        97.3      100.0     56.2

    marital  clients  actual yes%  selected%  recall%  precision%
    married   5,006       9.8       16.8       64.7     37.8
    single    2,307      14.3       28.8       74.8     37.1
    divorced    911      11.4       15.8       54.8     39.6
    unknown      12       8.3        8.3        0.0      0.0

  - Large differences in who gets called (61+: ~98%; 41-50: 12.5%).
  - They follow real differences in response (61+ subscribe at 41-55%),
    and precision is broadly similar across groups (32-56%), so the model is
    not simply wrong about these groups. But older clients would receive
    far more calls, and middle-aged subscribers are found less often
    (recall 54% for 41-50 vs 100% for 61+).

(b) Cost of removing age and marital (same RF settings, retrained on train)
                                       PR-AUC   ROC-AUC  top-20%
    RF all features                    0.4955   0.8137   0.6713
    RF without age & marital           0.4959   0.8140   0.6703
    difference                        +0.0004  +0.0003  -0.0011
  -> No performance cost at all.

(c) Does removing them change WHO gets called? (top-20% rule, both models)
    age     selected% with age   selected% without age & marital
    17-25        46.0                  43.6
    26-30        27.8                  25.4
    31-40        16.7                  17.1
    41-50        12.4                  14.0
    51-60        17.1                  16.5
    61-70        98.9                  98.9
    71+          97.3                  94.7
  -> Almost NO change. Other features act as PROXIES for age:
     73.1% of 61+ clients have job = "retired", and they were mostly
     contacted in the low-rate period (median euribor3m 0.85 vs 4.86).

  CONCLUSION
  - Simply deleting a sensitive column does not make a model "blind".
  - Recommended: drop age and marital anyway (data minimisation - zero
    cost), MONITOR selection and outcomes by group, cap contact frequency,
    and apply extra care for older (potentially vulnerable) customers when
    selling financial products. Human oversight of the call list.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. OVERALL CONCLUSIONS FOR THE SECONDARY LENS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. TIMING / ECONOMY is the biggest driver (55-63% of model importance):
     campaigns in low-interest-rate, low-employment periods convert far better.
  2. CONTACT HISTORY is the strongest client-level signal: previous success
     -> 65-77% yes.
  3. CHANNEL: mobile clients convert ~3x better than landline clients.
  4. WHO: students, retired / 60+ clients and singles respond more;
     blue-collar, services and middle-aged clients less.
  5. FATIGUE: 5+ calls in a campaign reduce the chance of success.
  6. The model's top-10% list converts at 53% vs 6.6% for everyone else.
